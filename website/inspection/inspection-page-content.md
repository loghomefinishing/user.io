## What every inspection covers (checklist)

1. Finish condition: UV graying, peeling, blotchy or thin stain, finish applied over damaged or "felted" wood from aggressive pressure washing, paint over logs.
2. Log joints and corners: open seams, failed or missing chinking and caulking, chinking stained over, wrong backer or three-sided adhesion.
3. Checks (cracks): size, depth, and especially upward-facing checks that act as gutters and hold water inside the log.
4. Log ends and corners: end-grain checking and exposure.
5. Bottom courses and sill: the splash and snow zone where rot starts first.
6. Rot: probed and mapped, from early surface decay to structural damage.
7. Settling, grading and drainage: erosion, joints opening as the house moves, water directed at the walls.
8. Decks, stairs, railings and support posts: rot at posts, stairs and rail sections, and whether they are safe to use.
9. Windows, doors and trim: failed seals, rotted casements, water tracking into the wall.
10. Roof edges, soffits, fascia and gutters as they affect the log walls.
11. Interior logs: water streaks, dirt lines from air leaks through unsealed joints, interior chinking.
12. Special conditions when found: water intrusion and mold, older coatings that may contain lead (lab testing recommended), and structural items such as a leaning chimney, which we refer to a structural engineer.

## Severity scale used in our reports

- Critical: active damage or a safety issue; fix before anything else (for example rotted stair stringers or deck posts, rot at the sill, active water intrusion).
- Major: will cause damage within a season or two if not addressed (failed finish, open joints, upward-facing checks).
- Moderate: maintenance items to plan for (worn top coat, small checks, minor caulking).
