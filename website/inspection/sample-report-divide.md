# Sample inspection report (anonymized)

A 35-year-old milled full-log home near Divide, Colorado. Inspection scope: exterior log walls, log joints, corners, and the sill and bottom log course. Inspected by Thomas Elliott, Log Home Finishing LLC.

## Summary of findings

This 35-year-old log home has never been properly sealed. In its entire service life the exterior has never received chinking or caulking at the log joints, corners, butt joints or checks, and the damage documented in this report follows directly from that.

- **Critical: rot is beginning** mid-wall at open joints and checks, and at the bottom of the wall where the bottom log meets the sill plate.
- **Major: no sealant anywhere on the log walls.** Every horizontal joint, corner intersection and butt joint is open to wind-driven rain and snowmelt.
- **Major: the existing finish has failed.** Stain is worn through to bare, gray, UV-damaged wood on the most exposed logs, and what remains is uneven and thin and no longer sheds water.
- **Major: logs are checked and cracked throughout.** Many checks are wide, deep and upward-facing, so they collect and hold water inside the log.
- **Moderate: the home has settled.** Erosion around the home has allowed movement that opened joints and stressed the logs further.

## How the home was built, and why that is not a seal

The logs were stacked with a gasket and glue between courses. That is a building process, not a sealing process. Both sit inside the joint, back from the weather face of the wall, and neither closes the exterior seam where water arrives. After 35 years:

1. Logs move and the glue does not. Logs shrink and swell every season; rigid construction adhesive breaks its bond.
2. Settling opens the joints. Joints that were tight on assembly day are now visibly open.
3. The exterior seam was left exposed. Rain runs down the curved face of each log into the seam below and sits on top of the gasket, inside the wall, where it dries slowly.

Modern practice treats the exterior sealant as a separate, required layer: an elastic sealant over backer rod on the outside of every joint, so it can stretch and compress as the logs move while keeping water out.

## Recommended work, in order

1. **Media blasting and sanding.** A new finish bonds only to clean, sound, bare wood, not to failed stain, gray UV-damaged fiber or surface decay. Blasting reaches the curved undersides, joints and the insides of checks; sanding follows to leave a uniform surface. Staining over the existing surface would trap the failed coating, and the new finish would peel with it.
2. **Six-coat exterior finish (Perma-Chink Systems):** 2 coats Prelude primer, 2 coats Lifeline Ultra-2 stain, 2 coats Lifeline Advance clear top coat. The system is breathable: it sheds liquid water while letting moisture vapor out of the log.
3. **Sealing joints and checks.** Perma-Chink Energy Seal over backer rod at all log joints, corners and butt joints, and Check Mate on large checks, starting with the upward-facing ones. Without this step, water keeps entering behind the new finish and it fails the same way the old one did.

## If the work is deferred

The rot is at an early stage, when it can be stopped by removing the damaged surface wood, sealing and refinishing. Left unsealed, each wet season carries more water into the same joints and checks, decay moves deeper, and the repair changes from refinishing to replacing logs at the base of the wall.
